🔍 How it works

How the plugin fits go-simulator

go-simulator is the host. It loads the scene, keeps the world (its entities and its environment) and moves the clock. The motion plugin is a separate program the host starts; they talk over standard input and output (go-simulator’s protocol).

  • At the start the plugin describes itself: its five rules, their parameters, what each one reads and what it writes. That description is also where the rules reference comes from.
  • The four environment rules read the environment and write environment: the sun direction, the wind, the clouds’ offset and the tide angle.
  • entity_motion reads only the entities with a bob, spin or path property and writes transforms.

A plugin never touches the renderer: it reads its copy of the world and answers with changes (go-simulator’s writing plugins). The renderer then draws the sky, the sway of plants, the clouds and the water from the environment, and the entities where their transforms put them. Shapes are in world units and fields in physical units, as go-simulator’s vocabulary lists.

How the rules are stepped over time

The example’s rules are on a realtime clock. Every frame the viewer advances that stage by the seconds that passed, times the speed:

  • Each switched-on rule gets one step: the host sends the stage time and the step in seconds, the rule reads its copy of the world and answers with changes. The host applies them and forwards them to any other plugin that reads them.
  • Pause (P) sends no steps; the motions stop where they are.
  • Speed (, .) makes the steps shorter or longer, so everything moves slower or faster together.
  • A toggle key turns its rule off or on. A rule that is off is not stepped at all: the tide stays at its angle, and the day cycle in the example waits for T.

The rules work in rates, not frames: the sun turns 2π × step / seconds_per_day, the tide 2π × step / period, a path moves speed × step. So a slow and a fast computer show the same scene after the same time. gusts and entity_motion keep their own count of the seconds they have been stepped, so a rule switched off and on again picks up where it stopped.

How the pictures were made

The pictures in these pages that show motion (the bay at the top of the README, the strips, the sky and the clouds, and the three entity panels) were drawn from the plugin’s own answers. A small throwaway program, kept outside the repository, ran the real plugin in a go-simulator host with examples/living-scene.yaml, switched the day cycle on with T as the viewer would, put a made-up bay in the world (a ferry on a looping path, a skiff on one that goes out and back, two buoys that bob, a mill that spins), advanced the clock 0.1 seconds at a time for two minutes, and drew where everything was.

They are SVG drawings with their own light and dark colours, not screenshots of the viewer: the repository has no example scene whose entities ask for a motion, and the look of the sky and the water is the renderer’s work, not this plugin’s. The diagrams (how it fits, the two kinds of motion, the steps over time) were drawn by hand in the same style.
