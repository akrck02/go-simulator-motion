🌊 Motions

A still scene feels like a photograph. The motion plugin makes it feel alive: the sun crosses the sky, the wind picks up and dies down, clouds drift past, the sea rises and falls, boats bob and mill sails turn. It works in real time, while you watch the 3D scene. It changes the environment (sun, wind, clouds, tide) and the transforms of entities that ask for a motion. Its rules belong on a realtime clock; the speed keys of the viewer (, .) and the pause (P) apply to them.

The environment

Rule What it does
day_cycle Turns the sun around the celestial pole once every seconds_per_day (120); the stars follow it, and the renderer derives day, twilight and night from the sun’s height
gusts Varies the wind’s strength (variation, 0.5) and direction (veering, 0.35 rad) around the wind the world started with; plants marked to sway bend with it and clouds drift
cloud_drift Moves the clouds with the wind (speed, 0.05)
tides Turns the tide angle a full turn per period (period_seconds; 0 = 12.42 h in proportion to seconds_per_day). The tide’s heights and phases come with the scene; the renderer raises the water from them

Each rule only changes what the world already has: with no sun direction or celestial pole there is no day cycle, with no wind there are no gusts, and so on.

The strips above are two minutes of examples/living-scene.yaml, with the day cycle switched on by its key. With seconds_per_day: 120 the sun goes round once. The wind swings around where it started and never quite repeats: it is a sum of three sines whose periods do not divide each other. The tide period is the 12.42 hours of the principal lunar semidiurnal tide (known as M2: high water twice a day, a little later each day), kept in proportion to the day, so 120 × 12.42 / 24 ≈ 62 seconds.

The sun turns around the celestial pole, so it rises, climbs and sets along an arc; the rest of the day it is below the horizon. The clouds move with the wind, faster when it blows harder: at no wind at all they still creep, at 0.2 × speed, and at strength 1 they move at 1.2 × speed.

Entities

entity_motion moves the entities that ask for it in their properties. glTF node extras arrive as properties, so a scene can ask for motions without any code.

Property Motion
"bob": {"height": h, "period": s, "roll": radians} Floats up and down with a slight roll (a boat)
"spin": {"axis": [x, y, z], "speed": radians per second} Turns around a local axis (a mill’s sails)
"path": {"points": [[x, y, z], …], "speed": units per second, "loop": true} Follows the points facing where it goes (+Z forward); without loop it turns around at the end

An entity takes one motion: bob wins over spin, spin over path. Entities added later by other plugins start moving as soon as they appear: the rule reads entities with those properties.

What each key is when it is left out:

Motion Key When left out
bob height 0.001 world units
bob period 4 seconds
bob roll 0.03 radians
spin axis [0, 1, 0], upright
spin speed 1 radian per second
path speed 1 world unit per second
path loop true

  • bob moves the entity up and down around where it rests and rolls it about its own X axis. The phase comes from where the entity is, so two buoys a little apart rise and fall out of step.
  • spin turns the entity about an axis in its own frame, from where it rests.
  • path places the entity on the route at a steady speed and turns it to face along the segment it is on. A loop goes back to the first point and starts over; without one, the entity comes back the same way. Where the entity rests is replaced by the route; its turn and scale are kept.

Use

cd ../go-simulator
go run ./cmd/simulator -rules ../go-simulator-motion/examples/living-scene.yaml scene.glb

examples/living-scene.yaml runs the plugin with go run; after go install the command can be just [go-simulator-motion]. During development go-simulator and go-renderer are used from the folders next to this one (replace in go.mod). More in getting started.
